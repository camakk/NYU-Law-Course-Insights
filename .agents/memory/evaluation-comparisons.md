---
name: Evaluation comparison policy
description: Why evaluation rankings use approximate response weights and preserve source records.
---

Treat course comparisons as summaries of subjective student evaluations, not objective proof of teaching quality. Use offering response counts as approximate weights and disclose that per-question response counts are unavailable and source averages are already rounded.

**Why:** The uploaded workbook reports offering-level response counts only. Equal weighting would overemphasize tiny sections; pretending exact question-level weights exist would overstate precision.

**How to apply:** Retain response-count context and small-sample caveats when extending comparisons. Do not treat missing ratings as zero, merge duplicate-looking offering rows, or silently repair source anomalies. The user's core requirement is professor results across all classes and teacher comparisons within each individual course.

For directory minimums, interpret “courses taught” as distinct course titles; section counts include repeats and are labeled “Times Taught” on the Professors page.

**Why:** Repeated sections should not imply a broader range of teaching experience. Separate measures let users filter on either breadth or number of offerings.

**How to apply:** Keep distinct-course counts separate from section counts, use “Times Taught” on the Professors page, and apply minimums to the selected semester's records.

In course teaching histories, a professor's displayed score belongs to the specific course and semester on that line, not their all-time score or latest score from another term.

**Why:** The user wants to compare how each instructor performed in that particular semester; reusing a lifetime or latest-term score would misrepresent historical results.

**How to apply:** Apply the existing response-weighted policy to multiple sections of the same professor–course–semester; leave unavailable teaching scores as N/A rather than substituting another term's rating.

Course-level top-professor comparisons summarize teaching effectiveness for that subject across semesters, unlike the semester-specific scores in Recent Professors.

**Why:** The user wants the table's top-three preview to match its expanded course comparison, not to rank instructors by their latest semester or by ratings from unrelated subjects.

**How to apply:** Keep the preview and expanded comparison on the same eligibility and ranking rules; do not reuse the recent-history score for this summary.

Titles containing “Clinic Seminar” must be classified as Clinic only, never Seminar. For other titles, retain the precedence Externship, Clinic, Colloquium, Seminar, then Course.

**Why:** The user explicitly replaced the earlier dual-label rule: clinic seminars belong exclusively with clinics, not with seminar filters or seminar teaching summaries.

**How to apply:** Apply the exclusive Clinic classification consistently to course records, directory filters, and professor summaries; titles with no indicator remain Course.

Anonymous post-exam feedback is attached to a professor–course pair across terms, and uses an optional 1–5 difficulty rating independent of the written comment.

**Why:** This is the audience's chosen grouping; the source has no course codes, and a course pair may recur in many semesters.

**How to apply:** Do not imply that new feedback belongs to a specific semester or exam offering.
